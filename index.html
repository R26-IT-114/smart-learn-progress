Smart Learn

AI-assisted learning support, designed with care.

Smart Learn: An Adaptive Mobile Platform for Neurodevelopmental Learning Disorders is a research-based digital learning platform for Sinhala-speaking children aged 6–8 years with specific learning difficulties.

Project ID: R26-IT-114 · Dyslexia · Dysgraphia · Dyscalculia · Working Memory

Explore research →Project documents

Research domain

Integrated learning support for young learners.

Smart Learn combines four AI-assisted learning components for Sinhala-speaking children aged 6–8 years.

Project Overview

Smart Learn is an AI-assisted, adaptive learning platform designed to support Sinhala-speaking children aged 6–8 years who experience difficulties in reading, handwriting, numerical learning, and working memory.

Its Dyslexia, Dysgraphia, Dyscalculia, and Working Memory components combine interactive game-based activities, personalised feedback, and performance monitoring. The platform is intended to support children while enabling parents, teachers, and educational professionals to monitor learning progress.

Literature Survey

Research across learning difficulties and cognitive development highlights early support, structured practice, multisensory learning, individualised strategies, child-friendly interaction, and continuous monitoring as important design considerations.

Research Gap

Sinhala-language resources that integrate reading, handwriting, numerical, and memory support are limited. Existing tools are often fragmented, insufficiently adaptive, and provide limited AI-assisted interaction or detailed progress records.

Research Problem

How can an AI-assisted, adaptive, game-based platform support foundational reading, handwriting, numerical, and working-memory skills for Sinhala-speaking children aged 6–8 years while providing personalised activities and meaningful monitoring?

Research Objectives

Develop four interactive components; integrate selected recognition models; provide Sinhala guidance and supportive feedback; personalise activities; record learning outcomes; provide dashboards; and evaluate technical performance and usability.

Research methodology

A component-based development approach

Four specialised learning modules are developed and integrated into a shared platform with AI-assisted interaction, adaptive support, and progress recording.

  1. Requirements analysis
  2. Activity design
  3. AI model development
  4. Application integration
  5. Adaptive feedback
  6. Progress monitoring
  7. Testing and evaluation

Research components

Four specialised learning modules

Dyslexia

AI-assisted Sinhala reading and pronunciation practice, beginning with a literacy pre-assessment that recommends suitable activities.

Activities

  • Sinhala letter recognition and letter–sound association
  • Two-letter and three-letter word reading
  • Pronunciation assessment and adaptive reading games

AI and monitoring

CNN-based Sinhala speech classification uses MFCC audio features for selected letters and words. The model covers 23 speech categories, with 3,312 augmented MFCC samples, 2,649 training samples, and 16 kHz audio sampling. Reported test accuracy is 89.74% for speech classification only; it does not represent children's reading improvement. Monitoring includes recommended level, responses, accuracy, scores, duration, trends, and practice areas.

Dysgraphia

Adaptive Sinhala handwriting intervention through game-based activities, Sinhala voice instructions, visual feedback, and rewards.

Activities

  • Guided tracing and independent letter writing
  • Two-letter and three-letter word writing
  • Ruled-line writing, mirror-letter correction, and drawing activities

AI and monitoring

A CNN-based Sinhala handwriting model evaluates selected letters across 15 classes. The dataset contains 1,920 handwriting images from 140 primary-school students, with 128 images per class; Python and FastAPI support AI inference. Monitoring records attempts, time, erase count, recognition confidence, and activities requiring more practice.

Dyscalculia

AI-assisted foundational numerical learning for children who experience difficulty with number skills.

Activities

  • Guided tracing and freehand digit writing
  • Sinhala audio number listening
  • Number sorting, sequencing, Balloon Pop, and number identification

AI and monitoring

A CNN recognises handwritten digits 0–9 from canvas input. The MNIST-based model uses augmentation and image preprocessing, including cropping, centering, grayscale conversion, resizing, and normalisation. Supportive feedback and monitoring record responses, accuracy, response time, attempts, and numerical areas requiring practice.

Working Memory

Adaptive cognitive learning games that practise visual memory, sequential recall, and information retention.

Activities

  • Sequence Recall and Memory Matching
  • Video Story Recall
  • Shape Drawing and Recognition with adaptive memory activities

AI and monitoring

Difficulty adjusts according to individual performance. A CNN-based model recognises and classifies shapes drawn by learners, with YOLOv8 included in the machine-learning stack. Sinhala audio guidance and interactive feedback support engagement while accuracy, response time, errors, attempts, completion, and trends are recorded.

Technologies Used

Platform technology stack: Frontend — React.js, JavaScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS, Framer Motion; Backend — Node.js, Express.js, REST APIs, Python, FastAPI; Data and authentication — MongoDB, Cloud Firestore, Firebase Authentication; AI/ML — CNNs, YOLOv8, MFCC, speech, handwriting, digit, and shape recognition; Deployment — Vercel and Microsoft Azure; Version control — Git, GitHub, and npm.

Expected Contribution

Smart Learn brings together four specialised learning components within an accessible Sinhala-language educational platform. It integrates AI-assisted interaction, adaptive educational activities, game-based practice, and performance monitoring across reading, handwriting, numerical learning, and working-memory tasks.

Scope note: Smart Learn is intended as an educational support tool and does not independently provide clinical diagnosis of neurodevelopmental or learning disorders.
